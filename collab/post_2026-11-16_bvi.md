## East-west backup: launch points to the British Virgin Islands

Rev E1, 300 voyages per launch point, launches 1 Nov 2024 to 15 Jan 2025 on that winter's weather, the same seeds for every launch point. "Success" means the boat reaches the BVI holding point (open water south of Norman and Peter Islands) or makes landfall anywhere in the Americas. **This is one winter only.** Two-winter runs follow on Wednesday.

| Launch → BVI | Success (95% range) | vs Gran Canaria direct | Median days (p10–p90) | Arrive in March | Reach the BVI holding point |

- **Go south before going west.**
  - Boats sailing straight from the Canaries towards the BVI sit in the light winds north of the trades. They take about 140 days and arrive in May, and 1 in 8 are still at sea when the weather data ends.
  - The traditional route, south to about 20N 28W first, is 10 points better and a month faster, and half its arrivals come in March.
  - Starting in Cape Verde, already in the trades, is best: 85 days and about 90%.
- **Mainland starts are poor.** Lisbon loses 1 boat in 5 aground on the Iberian and Moroccan coasts.
- **Weed and storms barely register in this window.** Doubling our Sargassum-belt fouling rate cost 1 point. The chance of passing within 200 km of a tropical storm is 0–2%.
- **For a March arrival:** launch from Mindelo in early December to early January, or from Gran Canaria (via 20N 28W) from about 10 November to 10 December.
- **The approach to the islands:**
  - Direct routes arrive from the north-east, and 13–23% of them pass within 1 km of Anegada's reef. The southern routes arrive from the south-east, and only 2–3% do.
  - About 10% of voyages also passed within 1 km of the rocks off Virgin Gorda's south end, so the two-winter runs use an approach further south.

## Cornwall: holding pattern on arrival

Randolph's rule is that on arrival the boat holds somewhere safe, but as close to the coast as possible. Rev E1, 500 voyages per holding point, 2023 and 2024 weather, the same seeds as our baseline (which stopped each voyage 40 km off Scilly):

| | Falmouth Bay (about 5 km off Pendennis) | Mount's Bay (about 6 km off Penzance) |
|---|---|---|
| Reached the holding point | 64.8% | 51.2% |
| vs stopping 40 km offshore (paired) | −6.2 points (−9.2 to −3.2) | −5.4 (−8.4 to −2.4) |
| Passed within 1 km of | the Manacles 18%, the Lizard 14%, Scilly 13% | Scilly 14%, Wolf Rock 9%, the Runnelstone 8% |

- **The last 40–80 km inshore is the riskiest part of the trip.** About 6% of boats that would have arrived offshore are lost or end up on the coast on the way in.
- Next we're testing holding points in open water 10–15 km off: the Lizard offing, and the mouth of Mount's Bay. A pickup boat can make that run out in under an hour. Results are due Wednesday.
- A caveat: our coastline (Natural Earth 1:50m) is coarse around the Lizard, and we don't model holding station after arrival.
