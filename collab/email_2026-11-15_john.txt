1. THE BOAT
- Rev D (Thursday): added an independent "limp-home" backup controller and its own satellite tracker, and potted the electronics. 62.0% success, +14 points over Rev C5 on the same weather.
- Rev E (Friday): a reliability package.
  - A bilge pump that keeps the boat sailing. In the Rev D runs, leaks killed boats through the safe-mode response, not through flooding.
  - Membrane vents, a pressure-test valve and a humidity sensor.
  - Sealed magnetic-encoder rudder servos, and a detent on the tail servo.
  - A fairing between the fin and the bulb, plus a weed-shedding manoeuvre.
  - Result: 28.5 kg, about $3,280 in parts, and 76.4% on the old route (+14 over Rev D).
- Rev E1 (Saturday), from Randolph's decisions:
  - 16 x 14 mm carbon tail boom.
  - Leak "safe" limit cut to 1.0 kg, because the sump only holds 1.2 litres.
  - 45 km drop point.
  - Result: 88.4% on the new route, against Rev E's 87.6% on the same 500 voyages. The changes cost nothing measurable.

2. THE ROUTE
- Destinations: we compared 13 aim points across Europe and Africa. SW Ireland and Cornwall came out on top; the Canaries and Morocco did worst.
- Launch points: we compared 12 points from Maine to Palm Beach, then the best launch/destination pairs over 2023 and 2024 weather:
  - Hatteras -> Cornwall: 68.4%
  - The old plan, Ocean City -> SW Ireland: 59.2%
  - Off Hatteras the Gulf Stream is close, so the boat is 200 km out in under 3 days instead of 7.
- Randolph changed the plan to Hatteras -> Cornwall on Friday.
- A catch: Diamond Shoals isn't in the simulator's coastline. From a drop just off Hatteras Inlet, about half the simulated boats passed within 2 km of the shoals. We moved the drop to 45 km out on the Gulf Stream's inshore edge (0% within 10 km).
- A dead boat drifting from there runs aground within 30 days in 2-8 of 32 tests, against 14-17 from the inlet.

3. ENGINEERING CHECKS (a "naval architect's review")
- Flooded stability: the boat still self-rights with 3 kg of free water inside (stability -11%).
- Fatigue over a whole crossing, using the sea states along the simulated tracks: the fin root, stern post and counterweight arm each use about 0.2% of their fatigue life per crossing. The old tail boom would have used about 130% (it would likely fail), so the Rev E1 tube was necessary.
- Steering: the autopilot rule as first written lost its course in heavy seas. It's replaced by a ~4-6 second wave filter. The rudder servos make about 2.9 million movements per crossing (3.6 million on a long one), so the bench life test is now 4 million movements.
- Speed sensitivity: a boat 10% slower than modelled loses about 0.6 points; 20% slower loses about 5.
- Simulator: 4.4x faster, with results checked identical. It also gained hazard "watch lines" and support for westbound crossings.

4. EAST-TO-WEST PLAN (new, Saturday)
- Route: the trade-wind route, Gran Canaria -> Barbados as the first pair. This is the route of the only small boat to finish the Microtransat: Oshen's 1.2 m C-Star, 127 days, September 2026.
- Launch window: about 20 November to 31 January, so the 90-125 day crossing arrives before hurricane season.
- Hazards: Sargassum weed (record years in 2025 and 2026), strong winter trades near the Caribbean, mostly downwind sailing, and recovery off reef coasts.
- Being simulated: six launch points (Canaries, Cape Verde, Madeira, Cadiz, Lisbon) and five Caribbean destinations, plus the northern route (Europe to the US) as a comparison.
