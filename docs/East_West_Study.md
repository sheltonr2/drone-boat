# East-to-west crossing study

*14 November 2026. Randolph asked for a plan for an east-to-west crossing: launch times, crossing times and city pairs. This is that plan. It doesn't change the eastbound plan ([Launch_Plan.md](Launch_Plan.md)); the questions at the end are Randolph's to decide. The simulations are queued (section 6) and their results will be added here.*

## Bottom line

- **Westbound means the trade-wind route.** The boat would go from the Canary Islands (or Cape Verde) to the Lesser Antilles: about 4,500–4,900 km great circle, and about 5,200–5,600 km actually sailed. The wind and current push the boat west almost the whole way. This is also the route of the only small boat to have finished the Microtransat. Oshen's *C-Star PC13* (1.2 m) sailed from Gran Canaria to Barbados in 127 days in 2026 ([Crossing_History.md](Crossing_History.md)).
- **The northern route (Europe to the US) is not a candidate.** It means beating into the westerlies and the Gulf Stream. Saildrone's 7 m SD 1021 did it in 68 days, but no small boat has. Run 020 will put a number on it for our boat, using the weather data we already have.
- **Launch window: about 20 November to 31 January.** Hurricane season runs from June through November. A launch after it ends, with a 90–130 day crossing, arrives in March–May, before the next season starts. Oshen launched in May and arrived in September, at the peak of the season, and sailed through a developing tropical storm on the way. A February launch is the edge of the window: its slowest boats would arrive in June.
- **Expected crossing time: about 90–125 days from the Canaries, 70–95 from Cape Verde.** These figures assume 1.0–1.3 kn made good along a route about 14% longer than the great circle (Oshen's ratio). The simulator will replace these estimates.
- **Suggested first pair: Gran Canaria → Barbados.** Las Palmas is the base of the yearly Atlantic Rally for Cruisers and has the boatyards and flights. Barbados is the first island on the far side, so a boat that is late or off course still has the whole island chain behind it to fall back on. The alternatives being simulated are Cape Verde (1,100 km shorter, but harder logistics) and a French island (Martinique or Guadeloupe, an EU landfall).
- **What is harder westbound:**
  - The boat sails mostly downwind, which is a wing sail's weakest point of sail.
  - Its course runs along the Sargassum belt, which hit record size in 2025 and its second-highest in 2026.
  - The "Christmas trades" bring 20–30 kn winds for days at a time near the Caribbean in December–January.
  - The far coasts are windward reef-and-surf coasts, so recovery has to happen offshore.
- **A timing point Randolph may want to weigh:** a westbound launch in December 2027 or January 2028 would come about four months before the eastbound May 2028 launch in the build plan, which is already in the build schedule.

## 1. The two routes

| | Northern route (Europe → US) | Trade-wind route (Canaries → Caribbean) |
|---|---|---|
| Wind | Westerlies on the nose; gales most of the year | NE–E trade winds astern, steady |
| Current | Against: the North Atlantic Current and Gulf Stream | With: the Canary Current, then the North Equatorial Current, about 0.7 kn westward |
| Distance | Cornwall–New England about 4,850 km great circle, much more sailed by tacking | 4,500–4,900 km great circle; about 14% more sailed |
| Precedent | Saildrone SD 1021 (7 m), Lymington–Newport, 68 days, 2019 | Oshen PC13 (1.2 m), Gran Canaria–Barbados, 127 days, 2026 |
| Season | Summer (Atlantic storms reach the western end Aug–Oct) | After hurricane season: launch late Nov–Jan |
| Verdict | Not a candidate; quantified by run 020 | **The westbound route** |

## 2. When to launch, and how long it takes

The constraint is at the far end: hurricane season (June–November) in the Caribbean. The table assumes 90–130 days at sea from the Canaries.

| Launch | Arrives | Hurricane season | Trades | Sargassum belt | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early–mid Nov | Feb–Mar | Late-season storms still form near the start of the route | Not yet settled | Moderate | Too early |
| **20 Nov–Dec** | **Mar–Apr** | Clear at both ends | Settled; Christmas trades near the Caribbean | Low | **Best**, and the classic yacht season |
| **Jan** | **Apr–May** | Clear | Strong, settled | Low, rising in spring | **Good** |
| Feb | May–Jun | The slowest boats arrive after 1 June | Strong | Rising | Edge of the window |
| May (Oshen) | Aug–Oct | **Arrives at the peak** | Steady | **Peak (June)** | Avoid |

Yachts on this route take 18–21 days; for scale, we are about six times slower. The Rally leaves Las Palmas in late November, and a second fleet now leaves in January. Both run at the same time of year that suits us, for the same reasons.

## 3. City pairs

The drop point is 40–45 km offshore on the downwind side of each launch island or port, clear of the island's wind shadow (the eastbound study found that close-in drops drift back ashore). The target is a few km off the windward coast. A landfall anywhere in the Americas counts as arrival in the simulator. The Canaries, Madeira, Cape Verde and Bermuda don't count.

| Launch (drop point) | Destination | Great circle | Sailed, est. | Days at 1.3 kn | Days at 1.0 kn | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gran Canaria (27.45N 15.95W) | **Barbados** | 4,772 km | ~5,420 km | 94 | 122 | Reference pair; Oshen's route |
| Gran Canaria | St Lucia | 4,876 km | ~5,540 km | 96 | 125 | The Rally's finish (Rodney Bay) |
| Gran Canaria | Martinique (France) | 4,838 km | ~5,490 km | 95 | 124 | EU landfall |
| Gran Canaria | Antigua | 4,815 km | ~5,470 km | 95 | 123 | Northern end of the chain |
| Gran Canaria | Puerto Rico (US) | 5,143 km | ~5,840 km | 101 | 131 | US territory; passes through the islands |
| El Hierro (27.35N 18.30W) | Barbados | 4,543 km | ~5,160 km | 89 | 116 | Westernmost Canary, quieter waters; fewer facilities |
| Mindelo, Cape Verde (16.55N 25.35W) | Barbados | 3,675 km | ~4,170 km | 72 | 94 | Shortest; already in the trades; logistics harder |
| Madeira (32.35N 17.25W) | Barbados | 4,912 km | ~5,580 km | 97 | 125 | Via west of the Canaries |
| Cádiz, Spain (36.40N 6.85W) | Barbados | 5,921 km | ~6,720 km | 116 | 151 | Mainland; adds about 3 weeks |
| Lisbon, Portugal (38.55N 9.95W) | Barbados | 5,862 km | ~6,660 km | 115 | 150 | Mainland; Biscay weather in winter is not on this route |

The "sailed" distances use Oshen's ratio: 5,419 km sailed against a 4,772 km great circle, a factor of 1.14. A mainland start adds three weeks at sea, and the first of those weeks are in the shipping lanes off Iberia and Morocco. That is where the eastbound study found boats get struck and picked up, so mainland starts are in the screen mainly to test that cost.

**Recovery at the far end.** The windward coasts of the Lesser Antilles are reef and surf, so a boat that lands there is likely damaged. Recovery has to be an intercept at sea from a charter boat, like the plan published for an earlier Microtransat entry, which would have met its boat off Antigua. That means two things:
- The autopilot needs an arrival behaviour: when it is within about 50 km, it rounds the island to the lee and holds station.
- The team needs a person on the island for the last week.

Barbados sits alone about 150 km east of the chain, so a boat that misses it still has St Vincent, St Lucia and Martinique downwind.

## 4. Hazards specific to the westbound route

1. **Sargassum.**
   - The Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt stretches from West Africa to the Caribbean. It peaks each year around June. 2025 was the record year and 2026 the second highest.
   - Our boat's main exposure is weed wrapping the fin and rudders. Rev E's weed fairing (T21) is meant for exactly this.
   - The simulator now raises the fouling rate inside the belt (3–22N, 15–65W) by month: ×2 in Dec–Feb, ×3 in Mar–Apr, ×4–5 in May–Aug. **These multipliers are guesses**, so one run doubles them.
   - A winter launch crosses the belt in its thinnest months.
2. **The Christmas trades.**
   - From December into February the trades near the Caribbean often blow 20–30 kn for days on end, with steep seas from astern.
   - Sea states along the westbound tracks get the same check the eastbound tracks got (run_seastates), once the tracks exist. This covers knockdowns, broaching, servo workload and tail-boom fatigue in following seas.
3. **Downwind sailing.**
   - A wing sail drives best on a reach. Dead downwind it is just a drag plate.
   - The autopilot already gybes downwind at the polar's best downwind angle, but the speed made good downwind is the number to watch.
   - The southern-route run (south to 20N 28W first, as sailors do) tests whether steadier trades are worth the extra 130 km.
4. **Hurricanes.** The storm-encounter check (HURDAT2 analog years, [Hurricane_Risk.md](Hurricane_Risk.md)) runs after each simulation, on a new tropical subset of the storm tracks (0–45N; the old one starts at 20N).
5. **Shipping and fishing near the start.** The Canaries sit on the Europe–South America and Europe–West Africa lanes, and there are fishing fleets off Morocco and Mauritania. The simulator's near-land collision and capture rates apply as they do eastbound.
6. **Solar.** At 15–28°N in December the sun is lower than on the eastbound route in summer, but the days are less cloudy. The power model runs on ERA5 radiation, so the runs will show whether winter energy is a constraint.

## 5. What the simulator needed

The simulator was written for eastbound runs. These changes make westbound runs possible. **For every existing (eastbound) run they change nothing.** This was checked before and after the change on three 12-day voyages on synthetic weather (with weed fouling forced on, so that code path ran) and on a draw of launch dates: identical output, byte for byte. The changed lines only take effect when a westbound key is set.

- **Arrival on the far side:** the scenario key `"far_side": "americas"` makes a landfall anywhere in the Americas count as arrival (Bermuda only if `atlantic_islands_count` is on).
- **Launch windows across New Year:** a window like 20 Nov–10 Feb now keeps its January dates in the following year, and the check for missing weather months looks into the next year.
- **Weather box:** `download --box` takes a different box. Westbound weather goes into its own cache (`simulator\workspace_ew`), so the eastbound months aren't mixed with a different grid.
- **Weed zones:** the scenario key `weed_zones` sets fouling-rate zones, with optional factors by month. Without the key, the old rule (Sargasso Sea ×2) applies as before.
- **Storm tracks:** `docs/data/make_hurdat_subset.py` builds the tropical subset of the NOAA storm tracks, and `stormcheck --storm-csv` uses it.
- **Scenario and batch files:** `collab/east_west/make_ew.py` writes them (launch points and destinations are in `collab/east_west/places.py`).
