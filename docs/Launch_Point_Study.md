# Launch-point study: where on the US Atlantic coast to launch, and the best launch and destination pair

12 November 2026. Plan, research and reasoning. Results will be added as the runs finish. Companion to [Destination_Study.md](Destination_Study.md).

## The question

The launch has been fixed 10 km off Ocean City, Maryland. Randolph asked on 12 Nov to test launch points up and down the US Atlantic coast, then combinations of launch point and destination, to find the best pair.

## What the research says

**1. The record of small robot boats points at the first few hundred kilometres.** The [Microtransat history](https://www.microtransat.org/history.php) lists every attempt.

- Eight boats left **Cape Cod** between 2014 and 2020. None got further than about 2,100 km. Three were caught by fishing boats within three to six days, and a fourth ran aground and was picked up by one.
- Three left **Gloucester, Massachusetts**. They looped, ran aground on Nantucket, or were withdrawn.
- Four left **Newfoundland**, which is already beyond the New England shelf and its fisheries. One finished (SB Met, 2018, 79 days), one reached the Irish coast after 6,041 km, one covered 4,682 km, and one sailed south and was lost.
- Scout, a 4 m solar boat, left Rhode Island in 2013 and was [lost after more than 1,300 miles](https://makezine.com/article/maker-news/scout-transatlantic-when-is-a-failure-not-a-failure).

The lesson is that boats die on the continental shelf, among the fishing fleets, before they reach deep water. Newfoundland is not a US launch point, but it shows what skipping the shelf is worth.

**2. Unsteered miniboats are launched into the Gulf Stream, not from the beach.** [Educational Passages](https://educationalpassages.org) has ships drop its 1.5 m boats in the stream. One boat shows why: Gryphon Cruiser was [launched off South Carolina less than 50 nautical miles short of the stream, and three days later it was ashore at Daytona Beach](https://educationalpassages.org/?p=15604). Relaunched from a ship, it strayed out of the stream and landed on Nantucket within six weeks. So a launch that stops short of the stream can end within days, and even the stream throws boats back at New England. Our boat steers, which should help with both.

**3. How far the Gulf Stream is from the beach varies enormously.**

- Off Jupiter and Palm Beach, Florida, it runs [1 to 3 miles from the inlet on many days](https://rigline-web-azure-mu.vercel.app/blog/best-offshore-fishing-spots-in-florida-data-driven-breakdown).
- Off Cape Hatteras it is [15 to 25 miles out](https://islandfreepress.org/?p=7099), the closest anywhere north of Florida, and charter boats fish it routinely.
- At Hatteras the stream leaves the coast. South of New England its north wall averages [240 km beyond the shelf break](https://scienceweb.whoi.edu/PO/linew/stream_coordinate_sections.php), which itself is 100 to 200 km offshore.

**4. On the Mid-Atlantic shelf the water moves slowly offshore and south-west.** Ten years of radar measurements ([Roarty et al. 2020](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/48639/noaa_48639_DS1.pdf)) give a mean surface flow of 2 to 12 cm/s, weakest in summer (3 to 6 cm/s). In the south of the region the flow turns offshore and joins the Gulf Stream near Hatteras. The day-to-day swings (10 to 20 cm/s) are much larger than the mean. So off Ocean City the shelf current neither helps nor hurts much: the boat has to sail about 250 km to reach the stream, in whatever the wind is doing.

**5. Early-season tropical storms form close to the southern launch points.** In June, storms tend to form in the Gulf of Mexico, the Caribbean or [off the south-east coast](https://michaelrlowry.substack.com/p/from-late-june-to-early-july-where), sometimes from [fronts stalled over the Gulf Stream](https://products.climate.ncsu.edu/weather/hurricanes/science). A boat launched from Florida or the Carolinas spends its first weeks there. Our weather data understates the winds in tropical storms, so the runs will flatter the southern launches on this point.

## What the destination runs already show about the launch

In the batch-1 Ireland run from Ocean City, 9% of boats were lost or aground within three weeks, 11% ended aground on the American side, and the median boat took 6.8 days to get 200 km from the launch point. So the near-coast phase costs roughly 10 points of success. That is the most a better launch point could win back from that phase alone; a faster crossing could add more.

**Autopilot change for this study.** Until now a waypoint counted only when the boat came within 25 km of it. In a 2 m/s current a boat can be swept past, and the old rule would turn it back against the stream. The launch scenarios use a new option (`"waypoint_rule": "abeam"`): a waypoint also counts once the boat has passed it. The earlier destination runs keep the old rule, so Ocean City is run again with the new rule as this study's baseline. Comparing it with the batch-1 Ireland run shows what the rule itself changes.

## What the simulator can and cannot tell us

It models, for every launch point: the real 2023 wind, waves and currents (currents at 1/12°, so the Gulf Stream and the Florida Current are resolved); land; and risks that rise near any coast (pick-up within 160 km of land, collision ×8 and fouling ×3 within 100 km).

It does not model:

- **Regional differences in fishing and traffic.** The pick-up risk is the same off Maine as off Florida. The Microtransat record says the New England and Nova Scotia shelves are far worse. The analysis therefore adds a sensitivity: each successful voyage is marked down for the days it spent on those grounds (Gulf of Maine and Georges Bank, the Scotian Shelf, the Grand Banks) at an extra 2% and 5% a day. Both rates are guesses. The Cape Cod record implies something near the higher one (3 or 4 boats caught in roughly 70 boat-days), but that is thin data from smaller boats.
- **Shoals and tidal races:** Diamond Shoals, Nantucket Shoals, Georges Bank, the Bay of Fundy tides. The land map has no shallows.
- **Lobster gear** in the Gulf of Maine, beyond the general near-coast fouling risk.
- **Tropical storms at full strength** (research note 5), which matters most for the southern launches.
- **Launch date.** Every launch point uses 1 May to 15 June. A Florida launch could go in April, before the storm season; a Maine launch might be better in June. Stage C can test this for the winners.
- **Cold.** Sea temperature off Maine in May is about 8 °C against 25 °C off Florida. That affects fouling growth, which is modelled, and fog, which is not.

Outside the simulator altogether: how far the boat has to be trailered, whether a charter boat is available (routine at Hatteras and Ocean City), and where a boat that washes back ashore can be recovered.

## What I expect, before the runs

- **Hatteras should do best**, and the 45 km charter launch better than the 10 km one. It is the only place where the stream is close and the route is not much longer. The risk is Diamond Shoals and the capes if the boat is set back ashore.
- **The offshore Ocean City launch** should recover most of the 10 points the near-coast phase costs, at the price of a charter.
- **Florida** gets a fast start but a 1,200 km longer route along a busy coast, within pick-up range for a week. I expect it to be close to Ocean City, and worse once storms are counted properly.
- **Charleston and Canaveral** have a wide shelf to cross before the stream. I expect them to be worst in the south.
- **New England** has the shortest route, but the model will flatter it because it does not know about the fishing fleets. Its fishing-adjusted figure is the one to read.

These are expectations, not results.

### What stage A shows

- **Going offshore beats moving along the coast.** The two charter launches top the table: 45 km off Hatteras, into the stream's inshore edge, 54.0% (+7.3 over Ocean City, range −3 to +17), and 100 km off Ocean City at the shelf edge, 50.0% (+3.3). Both nearly eliminate the near-coast losses: lost or aground in the first three weeks falls from 8.7% at Ocean City to 0.7% and 0%. And they cut fishing pick-ups (from 15 boats to 7 and 9).
- **Hatteras is the best beach launch**, as the research suggested: 49.3% from 10 km off, the shortest median crossing of all (107 days, against 121 from Ocean City), because the boat is 200 km out in under three days. Virginia Beach is about as good (48.7%).
- **None of these gains is certain on 150 voyages.** Every paired range for the top five crosses zero. The pattern (the closer the launch is to the stream, the better) is consistent, and it is what the research predicted, but the size of the gain needs stage C's larger runs.
- **New England gains nothing, and the fishing grounds make it worse.** Cape Cod (46.6%) and Montauk (46.0%) tie Ocean City in the model, but their boats spend 10–13 days on the New England and Scotian fishing grounds, against 4–7 from further south. With the guessed fishing mark-down they fall to 34–42%. Maine is worst affected: 24 days on the grounds, 22 boats picked up, and 18–30% after the mark-down. The Microtransat record from Cape Cod points the same way.
- **Florida doesn't pay.** Palm Beach is in the Florida Current from the first hour, but the route is 1,200 km longer and the boats take longer (127 days median). 42.7%, about the same as Charleston (43.3%).
- **New Jersey is clearly worse** (34.7%, −12, range −21 to −3): boats are carried north-east onto southern New England, Nova Scotia and Newfoundland, and 20% end aground (17% on the home side).

- **The single cells are noise.** The best cell, Hatteras 45 km → SW Ireland (54.0%), and the next, Ocean City 100 km → Cornwall (53.3%), each come with a poor cell from the same launch (46.7%, 45.3%). ## Recommendation (13 Nov 2026; adopted by Randolph the same day, with one correction)

**Update, 13 Nov evening:** Rev E from the 45 km point to Cornwall: **87.6%** (500 voyages, 2023 and 2024), +4.2 (+0.0 to +8.4) over the inlet, clear of the shoals; the 45 km point is the recommended drop ([Launch_Plan.md](Launch_Plan.md), [Naval_Review.md](Naval_Review.md)).

**Correction, 13 Nov, after adoption:** the 10 km drop off Hatteras Inlet sends about half the simulated tracks across Diamond Shoals, which the simulator's coastline doesn't hold ([Naval_Review.md](Naval_Review.md)). The provisional drop point is the 45 km charter point ("Hatteras 45 km" in stages A and B), which tied with the inlet on success and stays clear of the shoals. Runs to confirm it on Rev E are queued. The destination half of the recommendation (Cornwall) is unaffected. The text below is as written before the correction.

**Launch 10 km off Hatteras Inlet and aim for Cornwall** (Isles of Scilly / Land's End), instead of 10 km off Ocean City to SW Ireland.

- **How sure:** +9.2 points (+3.8 to +14.8) over the current plan on Rev D, the same in both weather years. That is the first launch result in this study whose range clears zero by a clear margin. Each half of it alone is borderline: the launch is worth about 5 points and the destination about 4.
- **Why it works:**
  - Off Hatteras the Gulf Stream is close. The boat is 200 km out in under 3 days (Ocean City: 7), so it spends less time near the coast, the fishing grounds and the shipping lanes. Losses fall from 15.2% to 6.8%, and the first-three-weeks losses from 7.4% to 2.8%.
  - It also sees slightly fewer tropical storms: within 200 km on 13% of voyages against 16%.
  - Cornwall is a slightly easier target than SW Ireland on the same tracks.
- **What it costs in practice:**
  - **Distance:** Hatteras is about 5 hours' drive south of Ocean City, plus a boat out of Hatteras Inlet. The inlet shoals and breaks in any swell, so go out on a calm morning with a local skipper, not a borrowed skiff.
  - **The charter alternative:** the 45 km charter from Hatteras did as well as the Inlet in stage B (tied), so if the Inlet is unworkable on the day, go with the Hatteras charter.
  - **Recovery:** Cornwall and the Scillies have good harbours and an active sea-rescue and fishing community for recovery. Recovery planning and contacts move from Ireland to south-west England.
- **What would change it:**
  - A test-season result from the real boat showing it can't hold its course in the Stream's eddies.
  - Real data on fishing-boat pick-ups, which the model only guesses. The Hatteras route crosses fewer grounds, so better data is more likely to strengthen this than reverse it.
  - Cornwall's approaches have heavy shipping; if a ship-strike model is added, rerun the destination half.
