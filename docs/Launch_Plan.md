## Boat and launch

- **Getting there:** Hatteras village is about 5–6 hours' drive south of Ocean City **(confirm from home)**.
- **Boat:** a Gulf Stream fishing charter out of Hatteras village. Those boats run 20–30 nm to the Stream as a matter of routine, and their skippers cross Hatteras Inlet daily. The boat must lift 29 kg over the side. A half-day booking should cover a 25 nm run out, the launch and an hour's escort **(confirm prices and whether a skipper will take it)**.
- **The inlet:** Hatteras Inlet shoals badly and changes fast. The Coast Guard has repeatedly warned of depths of 2–4 ft in its channels and of unreliable buoys. Don't take a borrowed or rented boat through it; go with someone who runs it every day, and ask about the inlet's state when booking. Oregon Inlet (about 90 minutes north) is the fallback port **(confirm)**.
- **Diamond Shoals:** keep the drop point and the boat's first two days well south and east of the shoals. The autopilot's first waypoint must not let the Stream carry it across them.
- **Weather to go:** winds under 15 kt and seas under 1.5 m at the drop point, no gale forecast on the Stream for 72 hours, **no strong northerly** (a north or north-east wind against the Gulf Stream raises steep breaking seas, the worst conditions the boat can meet on day one), and **no wind from the east or south-east for 72 hours**. In the drift tests from 45 km (13 Nov), four dead boats were back on Hatteras Island within two days; a dead boat drifts downwind, so those were onshore-wind days.
- **Escort:** stay with the boat for the first hour, until it is clearly holding course and both trackers have reported twice.
- **Early-failure recovery:** from 45 km out in the Stream, a boat that fails is carried north-east at 2–3 kn, away from the coast. That makes a recovery in the first week unlikely, unlike the Ocean City plan. This is the main thing given up, and it makes the coastal trial (Test F) matter more.
- **Drop procedure:**
  1. Power on and confirm a report from both trackers before leaving the dock.
  2. At the drop point, lower the boat by its lifting strop on the lee side with the charter boat stopped.
  3. Release it and escort it as above. Confirm the autopilot holds course and the next reports arrive.
  4. Log the time, position and photos.
- **Abort criteria:** no satellite report within 30 minutes, the boat won't hold course, or a leak alarm. Recover the boat and try again another day.

## Notices

None of these are permits for a vessel this size, as far as I can tell. They are notifications that avoid surprises and earn goodwill.

1. **US Coast Guard.** In 2026 the Coast Guard issued a safety alert after unmanned operations near port approaches began without notice. It asks operators to notify the cognizant Captain of the Port and the nearest Command Center before starting. Hatteras is in the **Sector North Carolina** Captain of the Port zone (Wilmington). Its Command Center has been listed as (910) 343-3880 **(confirm)**. Ask about a Local Notice to Mariners entry.
2. **Fisheries.** Cape Hatteras is the boundary between the Mid-Atlantic and South Atlantic fishery councils, so send a courtesy note to both and to the North Carolina Division of Marine Fisheries **(confirm the right contacts)**. The Hatteras charter fleet and marina notice boards reach the boats most likely to meet it on day one.
3. **United Kingdom.**
   - Send a courtesy email to HM Coastguard (Falmouth) and the Isles of Scilly harbour authority (St Mary's) before launch, and again when the boat is within ~50 nm, with live tracker links **(confirm addresses)**.
   - **Receiver of Wreck:** anyone who recovers the boat in UK waters, or brings it into the UK, must report it to the Receiver of Wreck within 28 days (Merchant Shipping Act 1995). The report is what gets it back to its owner. Tell the Receiver in advance that the boat is coming, and say so on the labels: row@mcga.gov.uk.
   - **Shipping:** traffic separation schemes lie off Land's End and round the Isles of Scilly. The boat can't follow the rule for crossing them, and the simulator has no model of ship traffic there. Aim for the west side of the Scillies and recover it before it reaches the lanes.
4. **France and Ireland.** A boat that misses Cornwall lands in Brittany or southern Ireland. Send the same courtesy note to CROSS Corsen (Brittany) and the Irish Coast Guard once the track shows which way it is going **(confirm)**.
5. **Microtransat (optional).** Entry is free. It requires a public tracker and an onboard position log at least every 6 hours. Our route isn't one of their standard start lines, so check whether they'd accept it **(confirm)**.

## Labels and marking

- **Both sides of the hull and the wing,** in large letters: "RESEARCH VESSEL – UNMANNED – PLEASE DO NOT REMOVE – IT IS NOT ADRIFT". Add a contact phone/email and "REWARD FOR REPORT". Repeat in French and Spanish for the European end. For a UK landfall add: "FOUND IN UK WATERS? REPORT TO THE RECEIVER OF WRECK (row@mcga.gov.uk) AND THE OWNER".
- **Colour:** fluorescent orange deck and wing panels (Microtransat suggests bright panels and warning labels).
- **Light:** the collision rules expect a sailing vessel under 7 m to show sidelights and a sternlight where practicable, or a white light. An all-round white LED at a few hundred milliwatts, switched on at night by the controller, is cheap power-wise. Add it to the power budget.
- **AIS:** a class B transponder needs a ship station licence and MMSI, and an AIS-SART is for distress only, so it can't be used as a tracking beacon. Skip AIS unless the power budget and licensing work out **(confirm with the FCC if wanted)**.

## Arrival and holding: first results (16 Nov 2026, run 021b)

Rev E1, 45 km off Hatteras, 500 voyages per holding point, 2023+2024 weather, the same seeds as run 017. Run 017 stopped each voyage 40 km off Scilly; these runs carry on to the holding point. Analysis: [`arrival_analysis.py`](data/arrival_analysis.py).

| | Falmouth Bay hold | Mount's Bay hold |
|---|---|---|
| Reached the holding point | **64.8%** (60.6–69.0) | 51.2% (47.0–55.6) |
| Success as scored (any European landfall counts) | 82.2% | 83.0% |
| vs stopping 40 km off Scilly (paired) | −6.2 (−9.2 to −3.2) | −5.4 (−8.4 to −2.4) |
| Of run 017's 442 arrivals: reached the hold / landed on the way / lost | 313 / 82 / 26 | 244 / 155 / 20 |
| Extra days for the last leg (median) | 3.3 | 3.4 |
| Passed within 1 km of | the Manacles 18%, the Lizard 14%, Scilly 13% | Scilly 14%, Wolf Rock 9%, the Runnelstone 8% |

**What this says:**
- The final 40–80 km inshore is the riskiest part of the voyage. About 6% of boats that would have arrived offshore are lost or end up on the coast on the way in. In Falmouth Bay most of those land on the Lizard; in Mount's Bay they end on the shore inside the bay.
- Both approaches bring a lot of boats close to rocks. A boat that reaches the coast counts as an "arrival" in the simulator, but on the Lizard or in Mount's Bay that is a wreck.
- **Recommendation (pending run 023b; Randolph's call):** hold in open water 10–15 km off, not inshore. A pickup boat can make that run out in under an hour. Run 023b tests two such points:
  - the Lizard offing, 12 km south of the Lizard and 30 km from Falmouth;
  - the mouth of Mount's Bay, 13 km south of Cudden Point and 17 km from Newlyn.
- **Limits of the model:** the simulator's coastline (Natural Earth 1:50m) is coarse around the Lizard, and holding station after arrival isn't modelled.

## Arrival and holding: offshore holding points (18 Nov 2026, run 023b)

These are the same runs as 021b, with the holding point in open water instead of inshore. Rev E1, 500 voyages each, the same seeds. Analysis: [`arrival_analysis.py`](data/arrival_analysis.py).

| Holding point | Reached it | vs the inshore point (paired) | Success as scored | Passed within 1 km of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lizard offing**, 12 km S of the Lizard (49.85N 5.20W) | **73.6%** (69.6–77.4) | +8.8 vs Falmouth Bay (+5.4 to +12.4) | 82.2% | Scilly 12%, Wolf Rock 3%, the Lizard 3%, the Manacles 0% |
| **Mouth of Mount's Bay**, 13 km S of Cudden Point (49.97N 5.45W) | 71.0% (67.0–74.8) | +19.8 vs Mount's Bay (+15.6 to +23.8) | 84.0% | Scilly 15%, Wolf Rock 6%, the Runnelstone 5% |

- Holding offshore gets far more boats to the pickup point, and almost none pass close to the Manacles or the Lizard. The last leg takes about 2.5 days instead of 3.3.
- Both offshore points still cost 4–6 points of success against stopping 40 km off Scilly. That's the cost of approaching the coast at all in the model: the simulator raises collision and capture rates within 100 km of land, and some boats still end ashore on the way in.
- About 12–15% of boats pass within 1 km of the Isles of Scilly on the way in. The first approach waypoint (7 km SW of Bishop Rock) is too tight.

**Decided by Randolph on 18 Nov 2026:**
- The **Lizard offing is the holding point.** It has the fewest close passes to rocks, and it's a 30 km run out from Falmouth.
- Keep the mouth of Mount's Bay as the alternative when the forecast favours it (17 km from Newlyn).
- **Scilly approach fix (approved and queued as run 025, `run_arrival_v2.bat`):** in 023b, a boat coming in north of the first waypoint counted it as passed once abeam, then headed straight past the islands. Two waypoints now bring every boat in from the south-west: 30 km SW of Bishop Rock (49.60N 7.20W), then 19 km due south of it (49.70N 6.40W).
- **Run 025 result (19 Nov 2026), 500 voyages per holding point, same seeds:**

| | Lizard offing, new approach | Mouth of Mount's Bay, new approach |
|---|---|---|
| Reached the holding point | **76.4%** (72.6–80.0) | 71.8% (67.8–75.6) |
| vs the old approach (paired) | +2.8 (−1.0 to +6.8) | +0.8 (−3.4 to +4.8) |
| Within 1 km of Scilly | **3.0%** (was 12.0%) | 3.0% (was 15.4%) |
| Within 1 km of the Seven Stones / Wolf Rock / the Lizard / the Manacles | 1.2 / 1.4 / 1.2 / 0.2% | 1.6 / 5.6 / 2.4 / 0.2% |

  **The fix works.** Close passes to Scilly fell by three quarters, and the other rocks are down too. Getting to the holding point is about as likely as before (a small gain within the noise), and the last leg takes about 2.9 days. **This two-waypoint approach is now the plan's approach.** The Lizard offing stays first choice: it has fewer close passes than Mount's Bay, where Wolf Rock is still 5.6%.

## The final plan on four years of weather (21 Nov 2026, run 026)

The decided plan run as one piece: Rev E1, 45 km off Hatteras, launches 1–20 May on 2021–2024 weather, the two-waypoint Scilly approach and the holding point in the Lizard offing (run 025's scenario). 1,000 voyages, 250 per year. Analysis: [`collab/runs_026/analyze.py`](../collab/runs_026/analyze.py).

| | Result (95% range) |
|---|---|
| **Reached the Lizard holding point** | **76.6%** (74.1–79.1) |
| Success as scored (any European landfall counts) | 81.0% (78.6–83.4) |
| By year, success / reached the hold | 2021 78 / 74; 2022 84 / 80; 2023 79 / 74; 2024 83 / 78 |
| Days at sea for arrivals | median 121, p90 145 |
| Passed within 1 km of | Scilly 5.4%, the Manacles 2.2%, the Lizard 1.6%, Wolf Rock 1.5% |
| A tropical storm of that year within 100 km | 1.0% (a hurricane 0.2%) |
| After the grade-D storm loss model (30% / 70%) | 80.8% |

**What it shows:**
- **About three boats in four reach the holding point, and four in five reach Europe.** That's the plan's figure. It's a little below run 019a's 86% because that run stopped 40 km off Scilly; the last leg in to the Lizard costs about 5 points.
- **Every year is close:** 78–84% success and 74–80% at the hold. No year is an outlier.
- **Storms barely matter in May:** only 1% of voyages met a tropical storm of their own year within 100 km.
- **Scilly:** 5.4% passed within 1 km, against 3.0% in run 025 (2023+2024, May–June launches). The extra years add a few close passes; the approach still works, but this is the rock to keep an eye on.
- The commonest problems along the way are weed or fishing line (0.29 per voyage), leaks (0.24, which the pump handles) and electronics faults (0.18).

**Decided by Randolph on 18 Nov 2026:**
- Keep 1–20 May as the target.
- Treat the whole span from 1 April to 15 June as usable. There's no measurable difference between windows, and every weather year scores 82–90%, so the launch date can wait for good weather at Hatteras instead of being forced.
- If the boat isn't ready by 20 May, launching up to 15 June costs nothing in these runs. The model scores late May–June slightly better (fewer boats still at sea when the weather data ends), but storm exposure is 2.6 times higher. The storm loss model is grade D, so that gain shouldn't be counted on.
- April is just as good at sea. It's colder for the launch crew, and the April weather at the launch site itself wasn't modelled.

## Open questions
- **Arrival behaviour: decided by Randolph on 15 Nov 2026.** On arrival the boat goes into a holding pattern somewhere safe, but as close to the coast as possible, for an easy pickup. Two candidate holding points are being simulated (run 021b, `collab/arrival/make_arrival.py`; 500 voyages each, same seeds as run 017):
  - **Falmouth Bay:** about 5 km SE of Pendennis Point, 20–30 m deep, sheltered from the prevailing W–SW. The approach keeps 7 km off Bishop Rock, 13 km south of Wolf Rock, 12 km south of the Lizard and 10 km east of the Manacles. Pickup from Falmouth.
  - **Mount's Bay:** about 6 km SE of Penzance, 30–40 m deep, downwind of Scilly in westerlies. The approach keeps 9 km south of Wolf Rock and the Runnelstone. Pickup from Newlyn or Penzance; it is open to S–SW gales.
  - The runs report how many boats reach each point, how many land on the way, and how close they come to the Seven Stones, Scilly, Wolf Rock, the Runnelstone, the Lizard and the Manacles.
  - Once there, the autopilot's station mode keeps the boat within about 1 km by sailing a slow box. The shore team chooses Falmouth or Mount's Bay from the forecast during the last week (superseded 18 Nov: the holding points are now offshore, see the table at the top). Shore team: send a Securité notice on VHF and notify Falmouth Coastguard of the holding position.

- **The drop point:** decided, 45 km (14 Nov). Ask a Hatteras skipper what the run costs and whether 70 km is a small extra.
- North Carolina: the boat has had no motor since Rev C, so state numbering should not apply **(confirm)**.
- Liability or third-party insurance for a drifting hull: worth one call to a marine insurer.
- Recovery in Cornwall or the Scillies: a local contact (a sailing club, the harbour office at St Mary's, a fisherman, or a Microtransat alumnus) willing to collect the boat.
- Customs: bringing the boat ashore in the UK is an import. Ask the Receiver of Wreck or HMRC what a recovered research instrument needs **(confirm)**.
